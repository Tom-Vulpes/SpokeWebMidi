The Amen break · The Winstons, “Amen, Brother”, 1969

In 1969 the Washington, D.C. soul group The Winstons put out “Color Him Father”, with an instrumental called “Amen, Brother” on the B-side. Partway through, drummer Gregory Coleman plays a four-bar solo lasting about six seconds. In the late 1980s it was reissued on the Ultimate Breaks and Beats compilations and sampled by hip-hop producers. Sped up and chopped into pieces, it became the backbone of jungle and drum and bass, and it is widely thought to be one of the most sampled recordings ever made. Coleman and bandleader Richard L. Spencer, who owned the copyright, were never paid for that sampling, and Coleman died homeless in 2006. Here the break is cut into 27 pieces, one for each pad on the SPOKE board.
